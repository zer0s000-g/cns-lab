### Frequencies

| Part | Band | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Localizer | 108.10–111.95 MHz | Odd tenths only (108.10, 108.15, 108.30 …), 40 channels. The even tenths belong to VOR. |
| Glideslope | 329.15–335.00 MHz (UHF) | Paired: tuning the localizer tunes its glideslope. |
| Marker beacons | 75 MHz | Beams pointing straight up. |
| DME (if fitted) | 962–1213 MHz | Paired too: 110.30 MHz goes with DME channel 40X. |

The simulator's ILS is **ICNS on 110.30 MHz**, glideslope **335.00 MHz**. The localizer sends its Morse ident on a **1020 Hz** tone.

### Difference in depth of modulation (DDM)

Both parts carry a **90 Hz** and a **150 Hz** tone. The receiver measures how strongly each is present (its depth of modulation, *m*) and subtracts:

<Formula>{`DDM = m₉₀ − m₁₅₀

Localizer:  20% each on the centreline; full scale at 0.155
Glideslope: 40% each on the glide path; 0.0875 at 0.12θ;
            full scale at 0.175`}</Formula>

Seen by a pilot approaching, **90 Hz predominates on the left** and **150 Hz on the right**. On the glideslope, **90 Hz predominates above** and **150 Hz below**. So a positive DDM on the localizer means "fly right", and on the glideslope "fly down".

### How wide is the beam?

The localizer <Term id="course-sector">course sector</Term> is sized so the needle reaches full scale about **±107 m (350 ft)** either side of the centreline at the <Term id="runway-threshold">threshold</Term>. With the antenna 1,000 ft beyond the far end of a 3,000 m runway, that is

<Formula>{`half sector = atan(107 m ÷ 3,305 m) ≈ 1.85°`}</Formula>

The sector is an angle, so the same distance off the centreline moves the needle more the closer you get: 100 m is less than one dot at 8 NM but almost full scale at the threshold. Outside the sector the DDM keeps at least **0.18** ("clearance"), so the needle stays pegged on the correct side.

### The glide path and its false twins

The glide path is about **3°** and crosses the threshold at about **50 ft**, so it meets the runway about 954 ft (290 m) beyond it. A 3° path at 140 kt needs about 740 ft/min of descent:

<Formula>{`descent rate ≈ groundspeed × 101.3 × tan 3°
(ft/min, with the groundspeed in kt)`}</Formula>

The antenna pattern repeats upward. In the simulator's teaching model

<Formula>{`DDM(e) = −A · sin(π · e / θ),   A ≈ 0.238`}</Formula>

it is zero at the real path θ, at **2θ ≈ 6°** (a false path where the needle works the wrong way round) and at **3θ ≈ 9°** (a false path that looks completely normal). There is no warning flag on a false path: the signal is strong. That is why pilots intercept the glideslope from below.

### Marker beacons

| Marker | Typical position | Tone | Keying | Lamp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer | 3.5–6 NM before the threshold (3.9 NM here) | 400 Hz | two dashes a second | blue |
| Middle | about 1,050 m (where a 3° path is about 200 ft) | 1300 Hz | dots and dashes | amber |
| Inner | about 300 m (CAT II and III runways) | 3000 Hz | six dots a second | white |

Many airports now use DME distances instead of markers.

### Categories and minimums

| Category | Decision height | Lowest <Term id="rvr">RVR</Term> |
|---|---|---|
| CAT I | 200 ft or more | 550 m |
| CAT II | 100 ft | 300 m |
| CAT IIIA | below 100 ft | 175 m |
| CAT IIIB | below 50 ft, or none | 50–175 m |

At the decision height the pilot must see the approach lights or the runway. CAT III approaches rely on autoland.

### Integrity: monitors, flags and critical areas

A monitor constantly checks the course and the signal strength. On a CAT I localizer a course shift of about **10.5 m** at the threshold triggers an alarm, and the ILS must switch off within **10 s** (CAT II 5 s, CAT III 2 s). The receiver then shows its **flag** and the ident stops. The simulator's ILS is certified for CAT III, so its monitor acts within 2 s.

Vehicles and aircraft near the antennas reflect the signal and bend the course without the monitor always noticing, so **critical areas** around the localizer and glideslope are kept clear whenever the ILS is in use, and a larger **sensitive area** in low visibility.

The signal is only guaranteed inside the **coverage area**: the localizer out to 25 NM within ±10° and 17 NM within ±35°; the glideslope out to 10 NM within ±8°.

<Note title="Honest simplifications">
The simulator uses a flat Earth near the airport, a simple straight-line model of the localizer inside its sector, the sine model above for the glideslope (held at full "fly up" below half the path angle), a made-up course error for the parked truck, a "fringe" of weak signal just outside coverage, uniform fog in which you see about as far as the RVR, a 15° cockpit cut-off angle and a simple autopilot. Angles off the centreline are measured from the localizer antenna; heights on the glideslope from the point where the path meets the runway.
</Note>
